# ATM Transaction Lifecycle

This document describes the stages an ATM cash withdrawal passes through in
TransactionSwitch, and which failures are possible at each stage. It is the
reference to reach for when a transaction fails and it is not yet clear which
component is responsible.

## The eight stages

| # | Stage | Owning component | Typical failures |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Card read | Terminal | Unreadable chip, fallback to magnetic stripe |
| 2 | Card identification | AuthorizationService | `AUT-2001`, `AUT-2004`, `AUT-2007` |
| 3 | PIN verification | CardSecurityModule | `CSM-3002`, `CSM-3005`, `CSM-3010` |
| 4 | Limit evaluation | LimitService | `LIM-4001`, `LIM-4002`, `LIM-4005` |
| 5 | Funds authorisation | CoreBankingAdapter | `COR-5003`, `COR-5008` |
| 6 | Dispense preparation | DeviceManager | `DEV-6002`, `DEV-6004` |
| 7 | Dispense and confirm | DeviceManager | `DEV-6009` |
| 8 | Completion or reversal | TransactionSwitch | `SWX-7001`, `SWX-7004` |

Stages 2 and 3 together are what the platform calls **card authentication**.
Everything from stage 4 onward happens after authentication has already
succeeded.

## Why a transaction can fail after card authentication

This is the most common source of confusion, because the customer has already
entered a PIN successfully and expects the transaction to complete. Once stages
2 and 3 pass, four independent things can still stop it:

1. **Limit rejection (stage 4).** The card is valid and the PIN is correct, but
   the request breaches a configured limit: daily withdrawal amount
   (`LIM-4001`), per-transaction amount (`LIM-4002`), or velocity
   (`LIM-4005`). Limits are evaluated against the account, not the card, so a
   second card on the same account can consume the daily allowance.
2. **Funds or core rejection (stage 5).** CoreBankingAdapter reports
   insufficient available balance (`COR-5003`), or the core banking system does
   not answer within `core.request_timeout_ms` and the adapter raises
   `COR-5008`.
3. **Device rejection (stages 6 and 7).** The amount cannot be made up from the
   notes available, a cassette is empty (`DEV-6002`), or the dispenser jams
   (`DEV-6004`). The authorisation succeeded; the hardware could not deliver.
4. **Downstream timeout (stage 8).** A component answered too slowly and
   TransactionSwitch abandoned the transaction with `SWX-7001` after
   `switch.downstream_timeout_ms`.

In cases 2, 3 and 4 the account may already have been debited, so
TransactionSwitch generates a reversal.

## Reversals

When a transaction is authorised but not completed, TransactionSwitch emits a
reversal (`SWX-7004`) to CoreBankingAdapter to release or refund the amount.

- Reversals are retried up to `switch.reversal_retry_count` times.
- Reversals are idempotent: the same transaction reference may be sent again
  safely.
- An unacknowledged reversal after all retries is written to the reversal
  exception queue and must be resolved by operations. This is the situation
  where a customer reports money debited without cash dispensed.

## Timeouts

TransactionSwitch applies a single end-to-end budget. If a downstream component
is slow, the switch abandons the transaction rather than leaving the terminal
waiting. The failure surfaces as `SWX-7001` even though the slow component was
AuthorizationService, LimitService or CoreBankingAdapter. Check the correlation
id in the switch log to find which call exceeded its budget.

## Correlation

Every transaction carries a `transaction_reference` generated by
TransactionSwitch at stage 1. All components log it. It is the key for tracing a
single transaction across services and for querying transaction status.
